

Paid Family Leave Version 1

Because New York has some of the strongest worker protection laws in the country, you likely have access to job-protected time off to care for your mother. If you drop everything and leave without notifying your employer or securing this leave, they could technically terminate you for job abandonment. You must formally request leave immediately.

Your Legal Protections in NYC

You have three primary safety nets that can protect your job while you are in Seattle. They generally require that your mother’s broken hip qualifies as a "serious health condition," which a major orthopedic injury requiring surgery or intensive rehabilitation absolutely does.

Immediate Steps You Must Take

Notify HR and your boss in writing immediately: Do not wait until you land in Seattle. Send an email explicitly stating that you are experiencing a family medical emergency because your mother has suffered a severe injury, and that you need to request family leave to care for her.

Request New York Paid Family Leave (PFL): If your employer is a private company, ask your HR department for the PFL request forms (specifically Form PFL-1 and Form PFL-3 for caregiving). You will need your mother's doctor in Seattle to fill out a portion confirming her medical status.

Ask for FMLA concurrently: If your company is large (50+ employees), ask for FMLA paperwork. FMLA and NY PFL usually run at the same time to give you maximum job protection.

Use your NYC Sick Time for the immediate transit days: Under the NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP), you can legally use your accrued safe and sick leave to care for a family member. This can cover you for the next few days while your long-term PFL or FMLA paperwork is being processed.

Important Constraints to Keep in Mind

If your employer threatens termination or denies your legally protected leave, you can file an immediate complaint with the New York State Paid Family Leave helpline or call 311 to connect with the NYC DCWP.

You can download official New York State Paid Family Leave (PFL) PDF forms directly from the NYS Paid Family Leave Forms Page or find specific form packets depending on your reason for leave.

Common PFL PDF Forms

Form PFL-1 (Request for Paid Family Leave): Must be filled out by you (Part A) and your employer (Part B, who has 3 business days to return it).

Form PFL-3 (Release of Personal Health Information): Completed by the care recipient to allow a health care provider to share medical info.

Form PFL-4 (Health Care Provider Certification): Completed by the family member's doctor for medical care claims

How to Submit

Complete the forms and gather required proof or doctor certifications.

Send the package directly to your employer's Paid Family Leave insurance carrier (do not mail them to the Workers' Compensation Board).

If you do not know your employer's insurance carrier, ask your employer, look at the workplace PFL poster, use the NYS Covered Employer Search, or call the PFL Helpline at (844) 337-6303.

Handling the Hoarding Environment

Hoarding situations require massive emotional and physical labor, especially when it threatens a 911 medical access pathway.

Create a "Clear Path" First: Do not try to clean the whole house when you arrive. Focus exclusively on clearing a 36-inch-wide path from her bed to the bathroom, kitchen, and front door so emergency responders can reach her if needed.

Professional Intervention: Look up specialized hoarding cleanup services in King County (like Bio-Clean, Clutter Trucker, or Steri-Clean Seattle). They are trained to clear hoarding environments respectfully and efficiently without causing additional trauma to elderly parents.

Since your father is post-surgery and headed to a nursing home, the hospital cannot legally or safely discharge him to an unsafe home environment.

Locate the Hospital Social Worker or Case Manager managing your father's care.

What the Law Requires

3-Business-Day Deadline: New York State law requires your employer to complete and return Part B of Form PFL-1 (which provides wage and employment info) to you within three business days

Employers Cannot Deny PFL: Your employer does not have the legal authority to approve or deny your Paid Family Leave claim—only the insurance carrier can make that decision.
